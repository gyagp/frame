# Subgroup Matrix — One subgroup. A different engine.

Audience: international GPU / WebGPU developers. English narration. 150 seconds,
1920×1080, 24 fps. Technical launch-film direction: restrained editorial typography,
macro chip imagery, precise diagrams and the actual supplied demo.

## Story

Question → mechanism → matched evidence → interpretation. The turn is the move from
lane-level FMA to a cooperative operation on distributed matrix fragments. The
ending answers the opening: same mathematical contract, a different execution path.

Considered structures: historical API chronology (too much context), a benchmark
countdown (would foreground numbers before mechanism), and a mechanism-to-evidence
journey (chosen). The source's eight slides become ten shots; no vendor history
table or isolated 22× case is carried into the short film.

Visual benchmark: the legibility and restrained diagram movement of polished
developer conference launch films. Learn hierarchy and reveal order, not a specific
brand's logo, score, shot or identity. No claim that a reference film was watched.

## Visual system

Deep graphite, pale lime and cool cyan. Inter typography. Deterministic matrix wipe
between scenes; restrained camera moves keep technical labels readable. Generated
silicon imagery is conceptual, not a photograph of the tested RX 9070 XT. Numerical
claims and equations are rendered as code-controlled text.

## Sound and timing

Azure Andrew Multilingual Neural: calm, clear and conversational developer documentary. Sentence clips
are timed after synthesis. Original 90 BPM electronic pulse and restrained harmonic
bed, with transition accents and quiet gaps before the cooperative and benchmark
reveals. Voice priority ducking; two-pass -14 LUFS target. Final timeline boundaries
are authored in `project.json`; this speech-led technical film does not force every
cut onto a musical downbeat. Captions burn in and are delivered as SRT.

## Evidence and review

Local demo and report are preserved with SHA-256 hashes in sources.json. Historical
numbers, preview status and landed-change claims are attributed to that supplied
snapshot, not independently re-benchmarked or updated to current release status.

Uncertainty to review: acronym pronunciation, pace of the API mapping shot, and
readability of the original dense demo. No claim of full human listening QA until
someone watches the finished film with sound.

Production gate: story review → keyframe approval → a roughly 12-second voiced
sample → sample approval → full film. The first project remains unapproved until
the user explicitly reviews it in the studio. No full render is represented as done.
