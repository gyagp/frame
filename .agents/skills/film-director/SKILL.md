---
name: film-director
description: Develop a film brief, narrative treatment, visual direction and purposeful shot plan for Movie Studio. Use for a new film, storyboard or substantial creative revision.
---

Read `projects/<id>/project.json` and the supplied source material. Preserve the
requested audience, language and runtime. Fill routine gaps with stated assumptions;
ask only for decisions that materially change the film.

Write `TREATMENT.md` around one subject, one goal and one turn. For an explainer the
turn may be a change of mental model, not a fictional conflict. Choose a concrete
opening in the first three seconds and an ending that answers it. Reference works
teach composition and rhythm; they are not templates to reproduce.

Give each scene a purpose, duration, framing, camera move and reason for the move.
Alternate density with breathing room. Prefer fewer on-screen words over faster
cuts. Technical evidence needs a locked, readable interval. Use a deliberate
signature move, such as lanes converging into one matrix operation.

Record palette, typography, transition grammar, sound palette and uncertainty.
Render representative frames with the actual engine before committing to a full
render. This studio's user explicitly requested story approval, visual approval,
then a voiced short sample before the rest of the film. Preserve those gates in
the UI and server. The user chooses the scene to sample. Reuse approved assets and
voice clips when continuing; substantive edits invalidate the affected approvals.

The studio's `project.json` is the executable timeline. `TREATMENT.md` explains its
intent; keep both consistent when the direction changes.
