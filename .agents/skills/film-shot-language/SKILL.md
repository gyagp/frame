---
name: film-shot-language
description: Translate a film's dramatic intention into purposeful framing, staging, camera movement, action and transitions. Use for shot design and revising the cinematic quality of a scene.
---

A shot is a change in what the viewer understands or feels. State that change,
then choose framing, spatial relationships, movement and a readable action chain.
Store these decisions in `scene.direction`, not only in an image prompt.

Use the original recipes in `src/engine/recipes.mjs` as grammar, not a fixed menu.
Choose a recipe for its effect: establish space, reveal a fact, follow a subject,
match a shape, compare evidence, anticipate an action, create depth, or allow a breath.
Do not count camera moves as a substitute for an intentional scene.

Write observable actions: actor → action → contact/target → reaction. Preserve
screen direction and give the viewer the space needed to understand a change.
Brief shots cannot carry unlimited actions. For abstract graphics, the actor can be
a shape, data point or signal. Plan a beginning pose, action and ending pose.

Compile the design through the data-only 2D engine. Prefer actual geometry changes,
paths and coordinated layers over a collection of static cards. For exact numerical
evidence, a held comparison can be the strongest shot.

Methods are informed by [the reference synthesis](../../../research/studio-references.md).
Do not claim to have reproduced the cited 424 techniques or 463 example videos.
